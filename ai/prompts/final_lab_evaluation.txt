### Evaluation Scope

Assess the section according to the following dimensions:

1. **Technical Accuracy:** Verify the correctness of engineering concepts, terminology, algorithms, formulas, programming explanations, and hardware descriptions.

2. **Implementation Fidelity:** Determine whether the described implementation, configurations, procedures, and technical decisions are supported by the available evidence.

3. **Experimental Data Fidelity:** Verify that measurements, observations, program outputs, simulation results, and test outcomes match the supplied experimental information.

4. **Programming and Algorithmic Correctness:** Where applicable, evaluate code logic, algorithm design, input/output handling, test cases, and computational reasoning.

5. **Hardware and System Correctness:** Where applicable, assess circuit descriptions, hardware configurations, component usage, connections, and measured behavior.

6. **Calculations and Mathematical Accuracy:** Verify formula selection, substitutions, calculations, units, numerical precision, and consistency with the supplied values.

7. **Results and Analysis:** Assess whether the results are interpreted correctly and whether conclusions follow from the available evidence.

8. **Error and Limitation Analysis:** Evaluate the identification and explanation of actual errors, limitations, inconsistencies, and uncertainty where relevant.

9. **Completeness:** Identify missing required procedures, implementation details, results, calculations, discussions, or conclusions.

10. **Academic Quality:** Evaluate clarity, technical organization, formal academic language, and adherence to the report requirements.

### Evaluation Rules

- Evaluate only claims and evidence available to the application.
- Never assume that code was executed, hardware was tested, or simulations were completed without evidence.
- Do not accept fabricated measurements, program outputs, benchmarks, test results, or experimental conclusions.
- Distinguish actual results from expected results and theoretical predictions.
- Do not require error propagation or physical measurement uncertainty for software-only laboratories unless the rubric requires it.
- Apply appropriate performance metrics to the specific engineering task rather than using irrelevant metrics.
- Identify critical technical errors separately from minor writing or formatting problems.
- Follow the supplied rubric and any explicitly defined scoring thresholds.

### Scoring Requirements

Assign each applicable rubric criterion a score from 0.0 to 1.0.

For every criterion:
- Provide a score based on the available evidence.
- Explain the reasoning behind the score.
- Identify specific inaccuracies, omissions, or unsupported claims.
- Indicate what needs to be corrected when a criterion does not pass.

Do not invent scoring thresholds or rubric criteria.

- `passed`: Use only when every required criterion passes.
